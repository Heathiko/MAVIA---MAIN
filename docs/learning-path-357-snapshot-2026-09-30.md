1. Reproduction in Flowering Plants
   Must learn first: -
   - [Reproduction-Among-Flowering-Plants-2 | lo809 | text] Reproduction in Flowering Plants
     Flowering plants reproduce sexually, which means a new plant begins when a male sex cell joins a female sex cell. Most of this activity happens inside the flower, which is the reproductive part of the plant. A single flower can contain both male and female parts, and these parts work together so that seeds can form. Seeds later grow into new plants, allowing the species to continue.

     A single flower can contain both male and female parts, and these parts work together so that seeds can form. Seeds later grow into new plants, allowing the species to continue. Male parts (the stamen) produce pollen; female parts (the pistil) hold the egg cells.

Below the anthers, and extending downwards, is a central, elongated structure – the pistil – which is composed of a sticky, top part called the stigma, and a shorter, tube-like section known as the style. 3. Stamen (Male Part)
   Must learn first: 1. Reproduction in Flowering Plants
   - [Reproduction-Among-Flowering-Plants-2 | lo810 | text] Stamen (Male Part)
     The stamen is made up of the anther and the filament. The filament is the thin stalk that holds up the anther, while the anther produces tiny grains called pollen. Each pollen grain carries a male sex cell.
   - [Reproduction-Among-Flowering-Plants-1 | lo821 | text] Stamen (Male Part)

4. Pistil (Female Part)
   Must learn first: 1. Reproduction in Flowering Plants
   - [Reproduction-Among-Flowering-Plants-2 | lo811 | text] Pistil (Female Part)
     The pistil, also called the carpel, is made up of the stigma, style, and ovary. The sticky stigma catches pollen, the style connects the stigma to the ovary, and the ovary contains ovules. Each ovule holds a female sex cell, or egg.
   - [Reproduction-Among-Flowering-Plants-1 | lo822 | text] Pistil (Female Part)

5. Petals and Sepals (Supporting Parts)
   Must learn first: 1. Reproduction in Flowering Plants
   - [Reproduction-Among-Flowering-Plants-2 | lo812 | text] Petals and Sepals (Supporting Parts)
     The colorful petals attract pollinators such as bees, butterflies, and birds. The green sepals protect the flower while it is still a bud. These parts do not make sex cells, but they help reproduction succeed.

     A gumamela (hibiscus) shows all the flower parts clearly, which is why it is often used in class. A mango tree flowers, is pollinated, and then forms the mango fruit with a seed inside.
