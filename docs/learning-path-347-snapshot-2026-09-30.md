6. Changes Caused by Heat and by Oxygen (Part 1 of 3)
   - [Lesson_Changes-that-Materials-Undergo | lo859 | text] Changes Caused by Heat and by Oxygen (Part 1 of 3)
     The Grade 5 competency moves from force to two conditions that cause deeper changes: application of heat and the presence or lack of oxygen. Heat can cause a material to melt, evaporate, soften, harden, or burn. Ice left on a saucer melts into water.
   - [Lesson_Changes-that-Materials-Undergo | lo860 | text] Changes Caused by Heat and by Oxygen (Part 2 of 3)
     Candle wax melts and then hardens again when it cools. Sugar heated in a pan turns brown and gives off a sweet smell as it becomes caramel — and that change cannot be undone. Oxygen is part of the air around us, and many changes depend on whether it is present.
   - [Lesson_Changes-that-Materials-Undergo | lo861 | text] Changes Caused by Heat and by Oxygen (Part 3 of 3)
     Iron exposed to air and moisture forms a reddish-brown coating called rust, which is why a bakal na pako left outdoors during the rainy season looks nothing like a new one. A candle covered by a glass jar goes out because the oxygen inside is used up. Sliced saging or mansanas turns brown when left on the table because its exposed flesh reacts with the oxygen in the air.

7. Reversible and Irreversible Changes
   - [Lesson_Changes-that-Materials-Undergo | lo862 | text] Reversible and Irreversible Changes (Part 1 of 3)
     By Grade 6, learners sort changes into two kinds. A physical change alters only the appearance of a material — its size, shape, or state — while the material itself stays the same.
   - [Lesson_Changes-that-Materials-Undergo | lo863 | text] Reversible and Irreversible Changes (Part 2 of 3)
     Physical changes are usually reversible: melted ice can be frozen again, a crumpled paper can be flattened, and dissolved salt can be recovered by evaporating the water. A chemical change produces an entirely new material with new properties.
   - [Lesson_Changes-that-Materials-Undergo | lo864 | text] Reversible and Irreversible Changes (Part 3 of 3)
     Chemical changes are irreversible: rust cannot be turned back into shiny iron, burnt wood cannot be turned back into wood, and a cooked egg cannot be uncooked. Useful clues that a chemical change has taken place are a new color, a new smell, bubbles of gas, heat given off, or a solid forming where there was none.

8. Useful and Harmful Changes (Part 1 of 3)
   - [Lesson_Changes-that-Materials-Undergo | lo868 | text] Useful and Harmful Changes (Part 1 of 3)
     The last Grade 4 competency asks learners to judge a change, not just describe it. Many changes are useful. Cutting cloth produces clothing. Hammering metal produces tools. Cooking makes food safe and easier to digest.
   - [Lesson_Changes-that-Materials-Undergo | lo869 | text] Useful and Harmful Changes (Part 2 of 3)
     Decaying leaves in a compost pit become fertilizer for a school garden. Other changes are harmful, especially to the environment. Rust weakens roofing sheets and bridges. Spoiled food causes illness.
   - [Lesson_Changes-that-Materials-Undergo | lo870 | text] Useful and Harmful Changes (Part 3 of 3)
     Burning plastic and other household waste releases smoke and toxic gases into the air, which is why the practice is discouraged and is covered by the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act. Learning to tell useful changes from harmful ones is what turns this lesson from a list of observations into a habit of responsible decision-making.
