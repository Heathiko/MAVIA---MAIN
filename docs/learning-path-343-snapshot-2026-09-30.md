3. Solutions: Solute and Solvent
   - [Lesson_Mixtures-and-Their-Characteristics | lo890 | text] Solutions: Solute and Solvent
     In a solution, the material that dissolves is the solute, and the material that does the dissolving is the solvent. In sweetened coffee, the sugar is the solute and the hot water is the solvent. The solvent is usually the component present in the greater amount, and water is so common as a solvent that it is often called the universal solvent. Three conditions affect how quickly a solute dissolves, and learners can test each one: Size of the particles. Finely ground sugar dissolves faster than a large lump, because more of its surface touches the water. Stirring. A stirred glass dissolves its sugar faster than one left standing, because stirring keeps bringing fresh water into contact with the solute. Temperature. Sugar dissolves faster in hot water than in cold water, which is why powdered drinks mix easily in warm water but leave grains at the bottom of a cold glass. Not every solute dissolves in every solvent. Salt and sugar dissolve in water, but cooking oil does not. Oil and water separate into two layers no matter how long they are stirred, which is why a bottle of salad dressing must be shaken before use.

   - [Lesson_Mixtures-and-Their-Characteristics | lo894 | text] Air as a Mixture (Part 1 of 2)
     Not all mixtures are liquids. Air is a mixture of gases, and the Grade 6 competency names its main components: nitrogen, which makes up the largest share; oxygen, which living things need for respiration; carbon dioxide, which plants use to make food; and water vapor, whose amount rises and falls with the weather.
   - [Lesson_Mixtures-and-Their-Characteristics | lo895 | text] Air as a Mixture (Part 2 of 2)
     Air is a uniform mixture: it looks the same throughout, and its gases do not settle into layers. Because the components are only physically combined, each keeps its own properties — which is why oxygen can still support burning even while it is mixed with the other gases.
